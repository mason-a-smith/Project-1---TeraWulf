# TeraWulf Inc. (Nasdaq: WULF) — Company Research Report

## Decision and information date

**Decision:** whether to authorize an initial investment in TeraWulf.

This report uses TeraWulf's FY 2025 Form 10-K for the year ended December 31, 2025, filed February 27, 2026. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), cover page and Item 15, p. 112.]

## Business

TeraWulf reports two operating segments: Digital Asset Mining and HPC Leasing. Digital Asset Mining earns digital assets through hash-computation services, and HPC Leasing provides high-density colocation to third parties. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 8, Note 19, p. 64.]

For FY 2025, TeraWulf reported $151.556 million of digital-asset revenue and $16.899 million of HPC lease revenue, for total revenue of $168.455 million. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 8, Consolidated Statements of Operations, p. 48.]

HPC leasing operations commenced in July 2025, and 18 critical IT MW of HPC leasing capacity was energized at year-end. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 7, p. 32.]

## Financial position and operating context

Cash and cash equivalents were $3.266 billion at December 31, 2025. Restricted cash was $456.386 million, comprising $189.933 million current and $266.453 million noncurrent. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets, p. 47; Note 2, p. 56.]

Debt carrying value was $5.171 billion: $46.316 million of current long-term debt, $489.767 million of short-term convertible notes, $3.052 billion of long-term debt, and $1.583 billion of convertible notes. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets, p. 47.]

Using unrestricted cash only, net debt is $1.905 billion, calculated as $5.171 billion of debt less $3.266 billion of cash and cash equivalents. [Source: Calculation from [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 8, p. 47.]

TeraWulf reported an operating loss of $186.213 million and a net loss of $661.416 million in FY 2025. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 8, Consolidated Statements of Operations, p. 48.]

Power cost was $0.060 per kWh in 2025, and energy expense represented 52.9% of the value of bitcoin mined. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 7, p. 30.]

Weighted-average basic and diluted shares were both 397.608 million in FY 2025 because potential dilution was anti-dilutive in a net-loss period. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 8, Consolidated Statements of Operations, p. 48; Note 2, p. 61.]

## Assessment and diligence focus

The investment case combines meaningful cash resources and an emerging HPC leasing business with debt carrying value exceeding unrestricted cash by $1.905 billion, early-stage HPC revenue, and a FY 2025 net loss. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 7, p. 32; Item 8, pp. 47-48 and p. 56.]

The principal diligence questions are whether contracted HPC cash flow can cover debt service and buildout spending, and whether bitcoin-mining economics remain resilient if power costs rise or mining conditions weaken. TeraWulf identifies HPC development, construction activity, and related infrastructure investment as its principal capital requirements; it also identifies bitcoin price, mining difficulty, global hashrate, power cost, and fleet efficiency as key determinants of mining profitability. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 7, pp. 28-30 and p. 27.]

The next diligence steps are to build a debt schedule from Note 10, underwrite contracted-versus-energized HPC capacity and buildout economics, model mining sensitivities, and reconcile liquidity against capital expenditure and debt-service needs. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 7, pp. 36-37; Item 8, Note 10, pp. 82-86.]

## Call

**Watch-defer; do not initiate a position yet.** This conclusion reflects the cited FY 2025 facts: early-stage HPC leasing revenue, $1.905 billion of net debt using unrestricted cash, and a $661.416 million net loss. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 7, p. 32; Item 8, pp. 47-48 and p. 56.]

This call would change if diligence demonstrates that contracted HPC cash flow can cover debt service and required buildout spending under stressed mining and power-cost assumptions. [Source: [TeraWulf FY 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000031/wulf-20251231.htm), Item 7, pp. 27-30 and pp. 36-37.]
